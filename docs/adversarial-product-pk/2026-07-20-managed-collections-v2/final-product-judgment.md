# Final Product Judgment

The work is accepted because the controller no longer treats a flat Skill name as ownership. The user correction is observable at every layer: declarative identity, immutable plan, apply precondition, mutation payload, status truth surface, live topology, regression tests and ADR language.

The final system does not pretend the machine is perfectly clean. It reports 47 collection-scoped collision observations over 37 physical locators with broken targets while keeping four owned collections ready. That is the correct separation: managed state is reconciled; ambiguous external state is visible and untouched.

Better Skills also demonstrates why “latest upstream” is a candidate, not a stable answer. The reviewed commit remains content authority, but the active selection is eight portable members. The invalid ninth member was neither hidden nor rewritten, and the prior generation remains recoverable.

The final challenger round rejected an earlier paper-only claim because live LangCraft was still plan.v2. That veto caused a real plan.v3 generation upgrade and exposed a second issue: predecessor-owned gateway links had been mislabeled as preserved collisions. The controller now excludes those owned exposures; the active LangCraft plan binds exactly ten foreign `prose-craft` paths, while their raw targets and mtimes remain byte-for-byte unchanged across the upgrade.

**Final judgment: READY within declared filesystem/current-Codex scope; no authorization exists to clean or reassign historical same-name locators.**
